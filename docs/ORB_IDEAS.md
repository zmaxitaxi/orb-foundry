# Next Classic orb ideas

Concepts only. These fighters have not been implemented or added to video pools.
Prioritize readable silhouettes, visible contact, short recovery, strong audio
identity, and counterplay. Every one inherits the shared approach/dodge charge.
Use seeded move selection and recent-move weighting; never choose a winner for
an animation. Avoid long freezes or repeated cutscenes.

| Orb | Signature moves | Animation and original sound | Counterplay / matchup hook |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Prism** | Catch one beam or projectile on a rotating face, then split the stored energy into three visible lanes. A missed catch sends a smaller shard fan. | Clear triangular core; a rotating white rim; rays separate cleanly. Glass resonance into a short crystalline chord. | Catch has a facing and a limit; body attacks bypass it. Can a beam beat its own reflection? |
| **Pendulum** | Plant a ceiling pivot and sweep a heavy bob across the arena. On the next move, release the pivot into a low rolling arc. | Thin chain and a large weight with an unmistakable swing shadow; damage follows the bob. Chain tension, low air sweep, deep stone strike. | The inside of the arc is a safe pocket. Can a quick orb get under the swing? |
| **Kintsugi** | Damage opens gold seams and banks a capped amount of energy. A visible crack threshold releases a ring of ceramic pieces, leaving a smaller exposed core until it reforms. | Simple porcelain body; few bright fracture lines; large readable pieces. Ceramic stress, a dry break, then a warm repair chime. | Banked energy is lost on a whiff; repair is not a free heal. Break it fast, or bait the release? |
| **Marionette** | Pin a thread between two walls and drag a hooked target along the real line. Cut the tension to fling both bodies in opposite directions. | One spool, two wall pins, taut thread, clearly extending hook. Tight string glissando and a sharp fiber snap. | A short hook window and finite pull distance; attacks can still interrupt. Can a heavy hitter turn the tug against it? |
| **Tidal** | Gather a low wave against one wall, then send a thick crest across the arena. A returning undertow pulls loose projectiles into a narrow second pass. | A single readable water arc with a foamy edge; the body flattens as the wave leaves. Water pressure, broad rush, focused splash. | First crest and narrow return have different safe angles. Can range survive a changing attack lane? |
| **Mantis** | Brace two visible forearms, hook past an opponent, then close the blades in a crossing cut. A miss converts into a short vault with no guaranteed damage. | Long articulated blades; pause before the cross; two fading cut lines. Dry joint clicks, split air sweeps, crisp blade contact. | A readable crossing point with a punishable landing. Can a shield catch both blades? |
| **Lantern** | Project a sweeping light cone. When its rim passes an opponent, leave one marked shadow; a later flare erupts only from that mark. | One swinging lantern, a restrained cone, and a single danger silhouette. Glass rattle, airy resonance, brief ignition. | Marks stay where placed; movement beats the flare. Can it lead a fast opponent into its own shadow? |
| **Jester** | Throw three visible jack-in-the-box traps with distinct tells: spring punch, ribbon snare, or recoil blast. Open them in a seeded order, then recover the empty boxes. | Simple split-color face and large folding boxes; each type has a distinct shape before it opens. Clockwork ticks, spring release, wooden knock. | No hidden trap type or instant damage; gaps between boxes remain playable. Can a cautious orb force the wrong box to open? |

Start with **Prism**, **Pendulum**, or **Kintsugi**. Each asks a clear matchup
question and produces a strong animation without adding screen clutter. Test
actual contact geometry, repeated move availability, shared dash inheritance,
seed repeatability, audio timing, and the portrait crop before video eligibility.
